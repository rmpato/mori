Install

Pick whichever you'd normally reach for.

$ curl -fsSL https://raw.githubusercontent.com/rmpato/mori/main/install.sh | sh

Installs to ~/.local/bin and adds it to your PATH if it isn't already there. Every download is checked against the release's sha256 before anything is written.

Options: --dir <path>, --version <tag>, --no-modify-path.

$ go install github.com/rmpato/mori@latest

This lands in $(go env GOPATH)/bin — usually ~/go/bin, which isn't on your PATH by default. Add it:

$ export PATH="$HOME/go/bin:$PATH"
$ git clone https://github.com/rmpato/mori
$ cd mori
$ make install

Go 1.26 or newer. make check runs the formatter, vet, and the tests.

Grab an archive for your platform from the releases page, unpack it, and move mori somewhere on your PATH.

$ tar -xzf mori_*_darwin_arm64.tar.gz
$ mv mori ~/.local/bin/

macOS, Linux, and Windows; amd64 and arm64.

Staying current

$ mori update

mori checks for a newer version, shows you what changed, and asks before replacing anything. The download is verified, and your current binary is kept aside until the new one is safely in place. It's the only command that touches the network.

Using it

Type mori and today's page is there. That's the whole idea.

Write

There's no "new entry" step — every day already exists, and most are simply empty. ↵ starts writing and esc stops.

Your words are saved when you pause, when you stop, and when you leave. Come back to a day hours later and mori stamps the time for you.

Look back

← → move through days, c opens the calendar, / searches as you type, # lists your tags.

? shows every key there is.

Your journal is a folder of Markdown

One small file per day, written atomically at 0600 in a directory only you can read:

~/.local/share/mori/journal/2026/08/2026-08-17.md

Tags are the hashtags in your prose; ## HH:MM marks a second sitting. No database and no index — if mori disappeared tomorrow, your journal would still be there.

It behaves in a pipe

When output isn't a terminal, mori hands you the file rather than a drawing of it:

$ mori show yesterday        # or: fri, -3d, 2026-08-17
$ mori search photography --json | jq -r '.[].date'
$ mori looking-back august
$ $EDITOR "$(mori path today)"

Every printing command takes --plain and --json.

What mori doesn't do

  • no accounts, servers, or sync
  • no streaks or word counts
  • no notifications, ever
  • no mood charts or trends
  • no telemetry
  • no subscription

Numbers appear in mori only when they're being interesting, never when they're being a scoreboard. The mood field is one optional word that shows as a quiet mark beside the date — no scale, no average, and mori never says anything back about it.

Open mori after three weeks away and it says welcome back. Once, without a number in it. There's deliberately no line for the opposite case, because that line is why people abandon journals.

mori doesn't ask how you're doing. It just leaves the page open.
